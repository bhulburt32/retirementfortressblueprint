Running a Fortress Blueprint

How to take a household from a list of statements to a client-ready Retirement Fortress design in about fifteen minutes.

Internal use Twenty-three sections, top to bottom No login, nothing installed
StartOrientation

What the tool does

You give it a household's income picture and a list of their existing accounts. It sizes the four Fortress levels from those numbers, then maps every account to a destination — which registration it consolidates into, which carrier it ends up with, and what stays put. Out the other end come five documents: three for the client, two for the file.

It does not replace your judgment. The allocation it proposes is a starting point you are expected to override, and section 14 exists for exactly that.

Nothing is saved by itself

The tool has no autosave. Close the tab or hit refresh and the case is gone. Every section header carries a Save button, so you never have to leave what you are working on. On the claude.ai demo there is no account: that Save (and Download case file in section 23) downloads the case file, and that file is the only way back. On Alto's own site you sign in and the same Save keeps the case on Alto's server under your name.

Everything opens with a +

The page loads with all twenty-three sections closed — a column of navy headers. Click a header, or its +, to open a section; inside, every subsection bar and every standalone table has its own + as well, and the year-by-year tables sit behind a + of their own. Expand all sections and Collapse all sections at the top do it in one go. Any Add a … button opens whatever it adds to, so a new row is never hidden. Every action button on the page is green; the only exceptions are the × that removes a row and the +/− toggles.

The page opens empty — a new case, every field blank, today's date in section 01. To pick up a household, press Upload case file in section 23 and open its case file (the .json or .txt from Download case file, or a Word .docx of the household), or on Alto's site press Load saved case in the top right. Start new case in the top right — twice, it asks you to confirm — empties every field again; your name and the date carry over, the client's details do not. There is no sample household in the page; a training copy can be opened with one by defining RFB_DEFAULT_CASE ahead of the script, which the repository notes describe.

LevelsReference

The four levels, and how the tool sizes them

The framework in one table — the same description appears in the Retirement Fortress Plan report. Send clients to the whitepaper for the full version.

Level What it holds How the tool sizes it
Roof Wishes Growth and legacy assets The residual — whatever the other three do not need
Walls Wants Balanced market investments The higher of 24 months of Needs, or (Wants less non-guaranteed income) ÷ the Walls yield
Foundation Needs Cash buffer, plus guaranteed income Buffer = months × Needs ÷ 12. Annuity = the Needs gap over the first 15 years of retirement — the highest month as a year, or the average, per section 03 (projected before the plan: Needs with inflation against Social Security with its COLA, pensions and annuity income) ÷ the Foundation yield
Moat Protection Long-term care contracts Benefit ÷ an age ratio: 3.2 at 55, 3.17 at 60, 3.1 at 65, interpolated for every age between and beyond

The Moat here is narrower than the framework's

In the whitepaper the Moat defends against long-term care and tax drag. This tool sizes only the long-term care piece. Withdrawal sequencing, Roth conversions and asset location are still your job, and the Retirement Fortress Plan report says the tax side is handled in how and when income is drawn.

02Input

Client information

Who the plan is for. Names print on every document and drive the filenames.

Client and spouse
First and last name, date of birth, age and sex for each. Enter the Date of birth and the age is calculated — whole years at the plan date in section 01 — and the age cell follows it; leave the date blank and the age is typed as before. The date also fixes the exact 59½ date for the early-withdrawal penalty in section 17 and feeds the Social Security optimizer and the pension calculator in section 04 when their own date is blank. Age and sex set the long-term care benefit defaults in sections 08 and 09. Leave every spouse field blank for a single client and nothing in the plan is titled Joint or Spouse.
State of residency
Sets the state income tax model in section 17 and is recorded for the file. Product approval is by state, so check it against the carrier before anything in section 20 goes to paper.
Dependents
Add adult dependent and Add minor dependent take a first name and an age each. They do not enter the Fortress sizing, but their ages drive the life insurance need in section 08 — years of income replacement and education funding — and they travel with the case so the file is complete.
03Input

Plan assumptions

The rates and choices the whole plan is built on, kept in one place so they are easy to review before a client sees anything. They are planning assumptions, not quotes, and the Retirement Fortress Plan's disclosures carry whatever you set here.

Liquidity buffer
Months of Needs held in cash. Defaults to 6. Sized on full Needs, not the gap.
Foundation sizing method and window (N)
How the Needs gap that sizes the Foundation annuity is measured, and over how many years of retirement: Peak monthly Needs gap in the first N years (the default) or Average Needs gap over N years, with N 10 by default. The tool runs the status-quo projection — nothing moved, no annuity bought — and for each of the first N full calendar years with everyone retired takes that year's Needs (growing with inflation, dated budget lines honoured) less that year's Social Security with its COLA, pensions and annuity income already in place. On the peak method the largest of those gaps — the highest monthly shortfall, taken as a year in the dollars of that year — divided by the Foundation yield is the annuity target, so a level payment covers the gap in every year of the window and runs ahead of it in the others. On the average method the mean of the gaps is used instead: a smaller target that runs a little behind the gap in the costliest years and ahead in the rest. Section 04 shows today's gap and the other measure beside the sizing figure, and the section 13 note gives the peak year. Shorten the window to size on the early years, lengthen it to cover more of the inflation drift.
Yields
Foundation yield and Walls yield, both 6.5% by default. The Foundation yield turns the sizing Needs gap into a Foundation target; the Walls yield turns Wants into a Walls target. Change them if the products you actually place justify it.
Guaranteed lifetime income
The default for every Foundation annuity in the projections; section 14 can set each contract on its own. A contract's value falls by the income it pays each year either way (no crediting is assumed). With Yes — the default, a lifetime-income contract — the payment continues for life after the value reaches zero. With No the contract pays a monthly share of its value and the payment stops once the value is gone, so Foundation income drops in the years that follow and the Roof carries more of Needs. Sections 15 and 17 and the Retirement Fortress Plan report all follow the setting; the sizing in section 13 does not change.
Growth rates
Walls growth rate (1.5% by default) is what the Walls balance is assumed to grow at after its income is drawn, and Roof growth rate (6.0%) is the Roof's assumed growth. Neither changes the sizing in section 13; both drive the projection chart there — Walls income rising with the Walls, and the Roof balance column.
Walls income start, growth before income starts, and the tax-free line
Walls income starts is the month the Walls begin paying (blank: the first retirement). Walls growth before income starts (5.0% by default) is the Walls growth until then; the Walls growth rate above applies once they pay. The tax-free line applies to Walls money in AUM - Tax Free (municipal bonds), whose yield is federally tax-free: Tax-free Walls yield (5.0% by default), Tax-free growth before income starts and after, each following the Walls figure while blank. These are plan assumptions for every scenario; the Walls income by account table in section 14 overrides them account by account.
Inflation
What Needs and Wants grow at in the projection. Defaults to 3.26%. The tax projection in section 17 indexes the brackets and deductions at the same rate.
End of plan age
The age of the younger client when the plan ends; 100 by default. It sets how far the income projection in section 13 and the tax projection in section 17 run.
Medicare
Medicare Part B and Medicare Part D, monthly per person. They default to the 2026 figures — the $202.90 standard Part B premium and the $38.99 Part D base beneficiary premium — and feed the Medicare lines in the section 05 worksheet. Standard premiums only: a household over the IRMAA thresholds pays more, so raise them for that client.
New custodian
Where Step 1 of the transfer sequence consolidates everything: Schwab or Fidelity.
Life insurance cash value
No leaves existing policies alone. Yes puts their cash value toward the Moat by 1035 exchange — and because a 1035 has to keep the same owner, each policy can only fund that person's own contract.
04Input

Income

The income side of the sizing — this section and the Needs and Wants in section 05 together drive everything downstream, so both are worth getting right before you touch the accounts.

It opens with an Employment income table, one column per person: annual salary, target retirement date, annual payroll deductions, the employee and employer retirement contributions as percentages of salary, which account from section 06 each lands in — the employee's contribution and the employer's match have their own destinations, so a Roth 401(k) deferral and a pre-tax match are kept apart (the employer's blank means the same account) — and the annual salary increase. The contributions are deposited into those accounts each working year in every projection, growing with them, and an employee contribution into a Roth account is taxed as wages; a line with no destination is treated as not saved, and the table says so. Beneath it the tool works out each person's net before-tax annual salary (salary less payroll deductions and the employee contribution), the dollars going into retirement accounts each year, and the time to retirement with the salary projected to that date. These describe the working years and are recorded for the plan; the Fortress sizing runs off the retirement figures that follow.

Under it, Additional employment income takes any number of further paychecks for either person — a second job, part-time or consulting work in retirement, a board seat. Each line has a name, whose it is, the gross annual amount in today's dollars, a start date (blank: already paying), an end date (blank: that person's target retirement date when they have one still ahead, otherwise through the plan) and an annual increase. The note beneath each line reads it back, and the hint totals what is paying now. These incomes count as wages on their own dates in the income gap, the tax projection, the status quo and the reports (they appear as rows on the Employment income table of the Retirement Fortress Plan and in the Household income tile); no payroll deductions or retirement contributions are assumed on them, and they do not move the retirement dates. Tick Self-employed on a line for consulting or practice income and the projections charge self-employment tax on it instead of the employee's payroll tax, deducting half of it from income. They save with the case.

Payroll taxes are taken automatically in every projection, so Payroll deductions is for benefits only: on each person's gross salary (retirement deferrals do not reduce it) and on the additional lines, 6.2% Social Security tax to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, indexed with the plan's inflation, 1.45% Medicare on every dollar, and the 0.9% Additional Medicare tax on household wages over $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (joint), which is not indexed. Self-employed lines pay 12.4% and 2.9% on 92.35% of the income, sharing the wage base with any wages. The payroll tax appears in the section 04 salary breakdown, as its own column in the cash flow and tax tables while anyone is working, and in the lifetime totals; the Fortress sizing, which runs off the retirement figures, is not affected.

Next comes Social Security and pension income. For Social Security enter each person's Primary Insurance Amount from their SSA statement, the age they will start benefits (62 to 70) and one COLA assumption (2.5% by default); the tool applies the SSA's own early-claiming reduction or delayed credits against a full retirement age worked out from the age in section 02, and shows the monthly benefit in today's dollars and what COLA makes of it by the start age. When the lower earner's PIA is under half of the higher earner's, the tool adds the spousal top-up — half the higher PIA less their own, reduced on the SSA's spousal schedule if claimed early, with no delayed credits — and notes that it is payable only once the higher earner has filed.

Under the Social Security table, the Claiming optimizer tests every claiming age from 62 to 70 (plus full retirement age) for each person — 72 combinations for a couple — paying each one out month by month to an assumed lifespan with the SSA's early-claiming reductions and delayed credits, the spousal top-up and deemed filing, and the survivor benefit (the larger of the two, with the 82.5% floor), COLA applied each January. It takes the PIAs, ages, names and COLA already on the page; you add only each person's life expectancy (blank: 85 for a man, 88 for a woman), an optional discount rate (blank ranks nominal totals; equal to the COLA ranks in today's dollars) and, the section 02 dates of birth (read-only here while section 02 has them; otherwise optional dates here) for month-level precision — otherwise ages are whole years from section 02. With Rank with the trust-fund stress test ticked the strategies are ranked on the 22%-from-2032 reduction used in section 19, with the no-cut total alongside. Tiles name the top strategy, its lifetime benefit, the surviving spouse's income under it, and where the plan's own claiming ages rank among everything tested; the table always lists the same six scenarios in the same order, each with its true rank among everything tested: 1 Highest lifetime benefit; 2 File late, both at 70; 3 File early, both at 62; 4 the client early at 62 and the spouse late at 70; 5 the client late at 70 and the spouse early at 62; 6 File at full retirement age, both at FRA (a single person gets 1, 2, 3 and 6; a person already past an age files at the earliest age still open to them; a scenario whose ages match an earlier one says so). Each shows the person's monthly amounts, lifetime totals and the age at which it catches up to filing earliest; a chart runs the cumulative benefits (the plan's own ages as a dashed line when they are not among the scenarios), and Break-even ages opens the full scenario-versus-scenario table. Use the top strategy in the plan sets the benefit starting ages above to strategy #1 (whole years), so the income projections, the Foundation sizing and every report follow. The optimizer's settings save with the case. Not modelled: the earnings test, tax on benefits, WEP/GPO, restricted applications, disability or dependent benefits.

Pensions are a list: Add a pension for each one either person will receive — a name, whose it is, the monthly amount in today's dollars, start date, COLA and the survivor percentage (0 to 100) — with a note under each line and totals by person. Other guaranteed income is a third list for annuities already paying out or scheduled to — Add an annuity income gives a name, whose it is, the monthly amount, start date, an end date or Life, COLA, survivor percentage and the account in section 06 that holds the contract; a contract whose end date has passed is shown but left out of the total. All three roll straight into the household figures the Foundation is sized from — there is nothing to copy or apply. A fourth list, Non-guaranteed income, records rents, royalties, part-time work or business income — name, whose (including joint), monthly amount, start, end or Ongoing, annual increase and a note. It never closes the Foundation gap, which only guaranteed income may do, but it does pay for Wants: the Walls are sized on Wants less this income, so a rental covering part of the discretionary budget shrinks the Walls target accordingly (the 24-month Needs floor still applies).

The Pension Benefits Calculator beneath the pension list estimates a public pension from the plan formula — the systems in Alto's Pension Reference Guide: PSERS and SERS, VRS, SRPS, TSERS, SCRS, TPAF, MTRS, MPSERS, TRS (Illinois and Texas), the three Ohio systems, PERSI, FERS and CSRS, and Wespath. Pick whose pension it is and the State / pension system, enter the date of birth and the expected retirement date — both come from sections 02 and 04 while blank, so the age at retirement is the plan's — and the employment start (the age at retirement and the years of service are calculated; type over the years if the record says otherwise), the beneficiary's age, then the Membership class / tier (the hire date suggests one), the average final compensation over the years the formula uses, any split-period service the class asks for, and the Benefit option. Calculate estimated benefit shows the monthly and annual benefit, the single-life maximum, the income replacement, the eligibility rule met, the formula, the system's COLA note and a warning where the system does not pay into Social Security (WEP and GPO). Add to pension income then puts a line in the pension list — the monthly benefit from the retirement month, the option's survivor percentage, COLA 0% until you set one — and the projections follow. The survivor factors are general estimates and every figure carries the reference tool's disclaimer: verify with the plan administrator before recommending an option.

The section closes with Your income today and in retirement, the same summary the Retirement Fortress Plan report carries: three tiles — household income today, guaranteed income in retirement and the share of Needs it covers — a table setting Social Security, pensions and annuity income against Annual Needs, and a Sources of income chart stacking salaries, Social Security, pensions, annuity and non-guaranteed income year by year as the plan stands today, before the Fortress is built. The note beneath gives the first full retirement year's figures. The Needs income gap the Foundation annuity is sized to close is shown in section 13, not here.

Carriers
There is no carrier field to fill. The Foundation annuity is assumed to go to the carrier of the largest annuity in section 06, and the Moat contract to the carrier of the largest life or LTC policy there. No such account, or no custodian typed on it, and the documents read "TBD" with a flag until you pick a carrier on the line in section 14 — fine for a first pass, not for anything you hand over.
05Input

Expenses

The section opens with the two figures everything is sized from, and the detailed worksheet folded away beneath them.

Annual Needs
Essential expenses — what the household costs before anything optional. This one number drives the buffer, the Foundation and the Walls floor.
Annual Wants
Travel, gifts, entertainment. What they would rather not cut first. The Walls are sized on Wants less any non-guaranteed income from section 04.

Type both straight in from a client's own figures, or press the green Complete Detailed Expenses bar to open Alto's monthly budget worksheet, line for line — six categories from Household to Entertainment & other, with three Other lines you can name and an + Add line button on every category heading for an expense the worksheet has no row for (a name, an amount, a Need/Want switch, dates and a note like any other line; the × at its right removes it). Enter monthly amounts. Each line carries a Need / Want switch, preset the way most households would answer (mortgage and groceries are Needs; dining out and travel are Wants); change any of them. Start and end dates shape the arithmetic: a line counts in today's Needs and Wants — the total at the foot of the worksheet, the figures it applies above, the Foundation sizing — only while its dates cover the month the plan starts, and the year-by-year projections carry each line on its own dates, so a mortgage that ends in 2031 drops out of the expenses then and a tuition line that starts next autumn comes in then. A line outside today's figures is struck through with a note under its date, and the footer shows the all-lines total beside today's. Notes are for the file.

Under Healthcare & insurance the worksheet carries six lines it fills for you. Life Insurance and Long Term Care Insurance take the monthly premiums of the policies listed in sections 08 and 09 (grey) until you type an amount — do that when the premiums are already in the budget. Prescriptions & Co-Pays carries a planning default in the same way: $600 a month for a couple, $300 for a single person, grey until you type the client's own figure (it follows the household in section 02, so it changes when a spouse is added or removed). The other four: Medicare Part B and Part D for each person, at the premiums set in section 03, with a start date of the month each person turns 65 worked out from the ages in section 02 (blank once they are 65 already). They are shown in grey while they are defaults; type over an amount or a start date and that line is yours and stops following. A single client gets two lines, not four. On their own the Medicare defaults do not count as a budget — the apply button waits for a line you entered yourself — but they are in the totals from the first line you add.

Or let the client fill in Alto's Monthly Budget Worksheet — the Budget worksheet button in the app's header saves the blank fillable PDF to send them — and Upload the completed Monthly Budget Worksheet — drop the PDF on the box under the toggle or choose it. The worksheet is a fillable form, and the page reads the form fields themselves, so a copy completed in Acrobat, Preview or a browser reads the same. Page 1 lands line for line in the detailed expenses: every worksheet line replaces what the budget held (a blank line clears it), the Need / Want switch keeps its preset, dates in the usual forms (06/2031, Oct 2027, 2027-05) become start and end dates and anything else is kept in the note, and a note on an Other line becomes that line's name. The Life Insurance and Long Term Care Insurance lines keep following the policies unless the worksheet gives an amount. Page 2 goes to Employment income in section 04, a column each: the payroll deductions (everything withheld except the retirement contributions) become the annual Payroll deductions, and the employee contributions and the employer match and profit sharing become the two percentages of the salary already entered there — enter the salary first, or the message tells you the dollar amounts it could not convert. Employer HSA contributions, pension accruals and IRA contributions outside payroll are reported in the message and not carried. The worksheet opens and the totals update; press Apply (or keep the sync ticked) for Annual Needs and Wants to follow it.

Under the table the budget rolls up into annual Needs and Wants next to what the two fields above currently hold. Apply to Annual Needs & Wants copies them up once; tick Keep Annual Needs & Wants in sync and every budget edit updates the fields as you type. Leave both alone and the fields keep whatever you entered. The green Complete Detailed Expenses bar folds the worksheet and its upload box away again with its +/−; the bar itself keeps the monthly total and the two annual figures in view, and a case with budget lines reopens with the worksheet showing.

The worksheet is optional

It is there for the household that has not done the budgeting work. Everything in it saves and reloads with the case.

Start and end dates are honoured in the projections. While the budget is the source of Annual Needs and Wants — kept in sync, or the two figures still equal to its totals — every year-by-year projection (the income gap, the status quo, the Fortress projection, the tax table) counts each line only between its start and end months, pro-rated to the month in the first and last year: a mortgage line ending June 2041 is half a year's payments in 2041 and nothing after, Medicare begins the month each person turns 65, and a line with no dates runs for the whole plan. The annual totals shown in the section, and the Foundation sizing in section 13, still use every line in full — so the Foundation is sized to today's Needs as before. Type over Annual Needs or Wants and the projections fall back to those flat figures. Debt maturities in section 07 are independent: end the budget line yourself when the loan clears.

The first plan year is prorated. The plan starts in the month of the Date prepared in section 01, so the first row of every projection carries only the rest of that calendar year — three months for a plan prepared in October — for income and expenses alike: salaries, Social Security, pensions, annuity and other income, the Walls yield and the Foundation annuity on the income side, Needs and Wants (and each dated budget line) on the other. Income tax for that year is still figured on the whole calendar year, since brackets are annual: the plan carries its share of the year's tax in proportion to its income (the rest was withheld before the plan), and any Roth conversion in that year is taxed at the margin on top of the full year's income — so the converter sees the real headroom, not a quarter-year's. The state tax is figured the same way. The section 17 tax table's first row is on that calendar-year basis: wages, Social Security, pensions, annuity and other income already in payment show the whole year's amount with the part before the plan date noted beneath, and Taxable SS is the taxable share of the whole year's benefit (“of $X for the year”). The months before the plan date also drew on the accounts — the household lived on something, and the balances in section 06 are already net of it — so for that year's tax base those draws are taken at the plan window's own pace (an IRA draw as ordinary income, a taxable-account draw as gain), noted under Withdrawals; nothing moves in the cash flow. The assumptions notes under the projections say which month the plan runs from.

Beneath the budget sits the Income Gap chart: every source of income as it stands today — salaries to each retirement date, Social Security, pensions, annuity and other income on their own dates — as a blue bar for each year, total expenses as a line, and the shortfall where expenses outrun income drawn in red on top of the bar, labelled every fifth year. The tiles above it give the year the gap first appears, the gap at full retirement, the largest gap and the total over the plan: the figure the Fortress is built to fund. It runs before any Fortress is built, so advisor adjustments in section 14 do not move it; the same chart is the Income Gap page of the Retirement Fortress Plan report.

06Input

Assets

Opening the section opens the account list with it. Three ways in. Pick whichever suits the case:

  1. Upload a spreadsheet. Press Download template for the Alto account template, fill it in, then drop it on the panel or use Choose file. You get a preview before anything lands — then Replace table or Add to table. CSV works too.

  2. Paste rows. Open Or paste rows instead and paste straight from a spreadsheet. Tab- or comma-separated, same column order, header row skipped automatically.

  3. Type them. Add account for one row at a time. Fastest for a household with four or five accounts.

Two columns decide more than they look like they do:

  • Tax type drives the whole waterfall. Qualified, Roth and Non-Qualified money go to different levels in a different order — get this wrong and the design is wrong.
  • Current position tells the tool what it is looking at. Mark bank savings and money market as Cash — that is what the liquidity buffer keys on, and a household with no Cash row gets no buffer. The five annuity positions — MYGA Annuity, Variable Annuity, Income Annuity (FIA), Growth Annuity (FIA) and RILA Annuity (a plain Annuity from an older case loads as a MYGA) — and the two policy positions, Cash Value Life Insurance and LTC / Life (handled alike: never drawn on, a 1035 into the Moat or left in place), get their own handling for assignments and 1035s, every annuity position lists its contract in the Annuities panel below, and each type follows its own rules in the projections (see the panel). An uploaded or pasted position such as MYGA, variable annuity, FIA with income rider, fixed indexed annuity, RILA or SPIA resolves to the matching type. An account set to Income Annuity (FIA) is always Foundation: it is already paying, so it stays with its carrier, counts in the Foundation and its income counts in the Foundation sizing, whatever its Transfer? column or its section 14 lines say.

The Transfer? column at the end holds an account back. Set it to Stay as is and that account still takes its place in the Fortress levels — the waterfall (or your section 14 lines) puts it in a level and the projections count it there — but it stays at its institution, generates no paperwork, and drops out of the billable base; the fee note names the amount so nothing hides. The one thing a held account never does is buy a Moat contract, which needs a 1035. (Stay put, no transfer on a section 14 line is different: that holds the money out of every level.) Transfer part opens an amount box: only that much of the account enters the plan and the waterfall; the rest stays where it is, held outside the plan at the account's own growth rate, shown as Held as is on the scenario cards and out of the billable base.

Excess cash flow, beneath the table, picks the account or accounts a year's income left over after expenses and tax is deposited into in the projections — after-tax money, so the list offers the taxable and Roth accounts. Several split it equally. With nothing picked the surplus goes to the household's Cash accounts (savings, checking, money market, in equal shares), shown with a dashed outline as the default; a household with no Cash account banks nothing until one is picked (spare RMD money is still reinvested in the after-tax Roof account).

The Cost basis column takes what was paid into each taxable account (non-qualified, not an annuity; other accounts show a dash, as their money is taxed on withdrawal). Left blank everywhere, half of each taxable-account withdrawal is taken as long-term gain, as before. Enter a basis on any taxable account and the taxable accounts carry their cost basis together, on average cost: a withdrawal's gain is its share of their value above basis, so the gain share rises as they grow; contributions, banked cash flow and reinvested RMD money add basis dollar for dollar. A taxable account still blank then starts at half its value (a Cash position at all of it, no gain); a basis above the value is treated as no gain. The gains feed the federal tax, the net investment income tax and the state model, and section 17's Capital gains column. The basis saves with the case.

The Growth column is required: the annual rate each account earns where it sits. It is what the projected assets before the Fortress (section 15) grow the account at, and what anything marked Stay as is keeps earning in the plan; money the plan moves grows at its level's rate from section 03 instead — the Walls rate for the Walls, the Roof rate for the Roof — whatever the account earned before. A new account has no rate (the cell is outlined in red and the total line counts the gaps), and until every account with a balance has one the asset projections wait: sections 15 and 17, the stress tests and the care-event projections show a notice instead, and the Retirement Fortress Plan, the initial observations and findings and the internal worksheet decline to build. Nothing is assumed for a missing rate — it counts as 0% — so enter the rate even when it is zero. The annuity rules still apply on top: a MYGA earns its own stated rate, a variable annuity the rate less its fees, an indexed or RILA annuity up to its cap.

Annuities
A panel under the accounts with one row per account whose current position is an annuity: the owner and account from the list, then Company (follows the custodian until typed), Product, Type (the account's current position — change it here or in the list, the other follows), Rate and Interest (deferred and accumulating, or taken as income) for a MYGA, Maturity date, Monthly income and Payout (single life or joint) for an income annuity, Fees as a percent a year, Cap rate for the FIAs and a RILA, Participation rate for the FIAs and Floor (the most a bad year can lose) for a RILA; cells that do not apply to the type are greyed out. The details save with the case and print in the worksheet under Annuity contracts.

The projections follow these cells. An income annuity in force is always Foundation: it stays with its carrier and counts in the Foundation (it sits on its own Foundation line in section 14 and under the Foundation in section 15); it pays its monthly income from now — the amount typed here, else a section 04 line linked to the account, else the Foundation yield on its value — for the owner's life when single (to the owner's end-of-plan age) or to the end of plan when joint, its value falls by what it pays and the income continues after the value is gone; the Foundation is sized net of that income; it takes no loss in the bad-years stress test. A MYGA marked Stay as is is different after maturity: it renews with its carrier at half its pre-maturity rate (half the typed rate, or half the plan rate when none is typed), still a fixed product that takes no loss in the bad years — in the status quo and the plan alike. A variable annuity is market money: it grows at the plan rate less its fees and takes the full stress loss. A growth FIA grows at the plan rate up to its cap, and sits at zero in a bad year. A RILA grows at the plan rate up to its cap, and in a bad year loses no more than its floor. What the tax figures assume

2026 federal law (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act figures), with brackets, the standard deduction and the capital-gains thresholds indexed at the plan's inflation rate; the Social Security thresholds are not indexed, so up to 85% of benefits becomes taxable over time. Wages, pensions, non-guaranteed income, tax-deferred withdrawals and RMDs are ordinary income; a tax-deferred withdrawal taken before 59½ carries the 10% additional federal tax (the birthday from the section 02 date of birth, or taken as mid-year without one; the withdrawal is grossed up for it, the table shows it in its own column and the assumptions line totals it); the Foundation annuity is taxed by its funding (qualified money fully, a non-qualified annuity half, Roth money not at all), and annuity income already in force the same way by the account that pays it — a section 04 line with no account, or a qualified contract, fully; a non-qualified contract half; a Roth contract not at all; a MYGA's interest fully unless the contract is Roth. The non-qualified share of the Walls yield is ordinary income (interest), unless the Walls yield is marked federally tax-free in section 14, and half of each taxable-account withdrawal is a long-term gain (or, with a cost basis entered in section 06, its share of the value above basis). The 3.8% net investment income tax applies to the lesser of net investment income — that Walls yield, the taxable part of non-qualified annuity income and those gains — and AGI over $250,000 joint or $200,000 single (not indexed); it is part of the federal tax and has its own column when any year owes it. RMDs follow the Uniform Lifetime Table from 73 or 75 by birth year; an RMD larger than the plan's own tax-deferred draw is taxed, spent first and otherwise reinvested in the after-tax Roof account. Both spouses are assumed living to the end of plan except in a projection that includes a death (the widow's or widower's curse above and the care scenarios in sections 06 and 19): the survivor files single from the year after the death, takes over the deceased's tax-deferred accounts (RMDs on their own age) and is the one counted for the age-65 deductions. No itemising, credits or IRMAA.

State tax follows the state in section 02 through a planning model of each state's 2025 rates and brackets, standard deduction or personal exemptions, and retirement-income rules — Pennsylvania, Illinois and Mississippi leave pension, IRA and annuity income untaxed; New York, Georgia, South Carolina, Maryland and others exclude a set amount per person from a given age; Social Security is exempt except where the state taxes it. Nine states have no income tax. Local income taxes (Maryland counties, Ohio and Pennsylvania municipalities, New York City) are not included. The whole projection is a planning estimate, not tax advice, and prints in the worksheet under Projected income tax.

NotesWorth knowing

Things that catch people out

  • No Cash row, no buffer. The liquidity buffer only funds from accounts marked Cash. If the household's savings came in as Market, the buffer sits empty and the Foundation looks wrong.
  • The LTC cost defaults fight you until you type. Changing a person's age or sex rewrites their average LTC cost — but only until you have edited that field yourself, after which your figure sticks.
  • Yields are assumptions you are publishing. Whatever you set appears in the client summary's disclosures as an assumed rate. Set them to something you can defend.
  • Held-back accounts are out of the fee base, not out of the average. An account marked Stay as is, or the part held back from a partial transfer, is out of billable AUM but stays in the weighted-average denominator: the weighted average is the advisory cost spread across every account the client holds, so holding money out lowers that percentage rather than raising it.
  • An account named "Joint brokerage" keeps that name. The tool never titles a registration Joint for a single client, but it will not rename an account you typed. Rename it in section 06 if it should not appear that way on a client document.
  • Refresh is fatal. Worth saying twice.
